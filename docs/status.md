- Built: onboarding form and results page, in `web/`, with live audience scoring and competitor research. Placement pricing remains mocked. The form is one fill-in-the-blank sentence ending in a city menu whose only city is New York, followed by New York's five boroughs as a fixed list. The results are a map of New York's census tracts with Customers and Competitors tabs, a Gap tab once qualifying campaigns are found, a legend and clickable campaign markers at matched stations, addresses and approximate areas. Each marker shows its competitor's logo, loaded from the competitor's own website, and falls back to initials when the site's icon cannot be fetched. Solid markers identify current campaigns with a station or address match; dashed markers identify approximate, historical or undated campaigns. Markers open no popups: hovering a campaign marker highlights its row in the Competitors tab, clicking it switches to that tab and scrolls to the row, and clicking a row's location moves the map to the marker. Each mapped row shows the data-source attribution. The placements to buy are numbered markers that match their numbers in the list, shown on the Gap and Competitors layers. Without a Gap tab they also show on the Customers layer, where they are listed; with one, the Customers layer omits them. They are chosen from observed gaps when current campaign locations are available, or from audience fit otherwise. Hovering a placement on the map or in the list highlights it in both, clicking a row moves the map to it, and clicking a marker scrolls the list to its row. Tabs above the results column pick the layer for both the map and the column, in the order Customers, Competitors, Gap. Gap appears only once a current campaign has a station or address match. Customers opens at the start; when Gap appears it opens by itself unless another tab was clicked. Switching tabs slides the highlighted tab across, and the map's colours change at once. The column shows only that layer's sections: Gap has the 5 biggest observed gaps and up to 12 placements to buy, Customers has the 5 tracts with the most target customers, the up to 12 placements when there is no Gap tab, and the weighted tract columns, strongest first, with those that count against the customer marked "less likely", and Competitors has the competitors, each with its logo on the right of the row, and their ads, with the agent's latest step while it searches and a note counting ads without a reliable location match and so absent from the map. Hovering a tract on the map names it and shows the current layer's value for it, and hovering a trait under Customers shows its exact weight in place of its measure. On the form, the whole background spins once every 30 seconds, sweeping colour through the edges the form card leaves visible, and its four coloured blobs each drift on their own loop of 7 to 10 seconds, moving up to 50% of the screen's larger side and growing by up to 40%. Real: the audience fit scores are weighted sums of the real scaled tract columns, and the placements are real kiosks and shelters selected across eligible tracts in descending observed gap-score order when current campaigns have precise locations, or descending audience fit otherwise, with one per tract and at least 500 metres between them. The column weights come from Jev through AI Gateway when `AI_GATEWAY_API_KEY` is set in `web/.env.local`, or on a Vercel deployment through its OIDC token: one request asks a five-level score question per column, from strongly against to strongly for, and each weight runs from -1 to 1, the probability-weighted level. Without either, or when Jev fails or takes over 20 seconds, the weights come from keyword matching on the target customer, and the Customers tab says so. Verified on 8 November 2026 with the sample brief: the Customers tab said "weighted by Jev", and the scoring request, Jev plus the tract arithmetic, took 892 ms. For "young professionals in tech who rent with roommates", the strongest weights were jobs in information (+0.94), residents aged 20 to 34 (+0.89) and median age (-0.83). Jev isn't on AI Gateway's free tier, so the key's Vercel team needs purchased AI Gateway Credits, and a card Vercel can verify. - Built: competitor research with the Vercel AI SDK's `ToolLoopAgent`, OpenAI directly through `OPENAI_API_KEY`, and hosted web search and page reading. The research default is `gpt-5.6-luna`, selected in `AI_MODELS` in `web/src/lib/server/ai.ts`. Model names are not read from environment variables. Typed tools report up to three competitors and six campaign locations, rejecting URLs absent from the run's research sources. Discovery has six model steps and a 35-second deadline. Each discovered competitor then gets six model steps and 65 seconds, with up to three searches running concurrently within a 165-second total deadline; the Route Handler has a 170-second deadline. Each competitor can contribute up to two campaign locations. The first two model steps require hosted search, with query plans for subway, billboard, bus shelter and LinkNYC advertising, then agency and media-owner case studies. Hosted-search-only responses continue into reporting within the same step budget. The model extracts the evidence; source membership does not independently fact-check its summary. Dates are classified as current, historical or unknown. Only matched subway stations or street addresses with a confirmed date range covering today affect competition. Geocoded areas and unknown or historical dates remain visible as markers without lowering gap scores. Other findings stay visible with source links, evidence and locations. A search returning no campaigns does not establish that competitors are absent. The sample recording contains three competitors and no verified campaigns. Re-capture it from `tools/` with `uv run python -c 'from tools.sources.competitor_run import load; load(refresh=True)'` while the web app runs.
