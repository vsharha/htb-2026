The results are a map of New York's census tracts with Customers and Competitors tabs, a Gap tab once qualifying campaigns are found, a legend and clickable campaign markers at matched stations, addresses and approximate areas. Each marker shows its competitor's logo, loaded from the competitor's own website, and falls back to initials when the site's icon cannot be fetched; each campaign row in the Competitors tab shows the same logo. Solid markers identify current or recent campaigns with a station or address match; dashed markers identify approximate, historical or undated campaigns. Markers open no popups: hovering a campaign marker highlights its row in the Competitors tab, clicking it switches to that tab and scrolls to the row, and clicking a row's location moves the map to the marker. Without a Gap tab they also show on the Customers layer, where they are listed; with one, the Customers layer omits them. They are chosen from observed gaps when locations of campaigns from the past 12 months are available, or from audience fit otherwise. Hovering a placement on the map or in the list highlights it in both, clicking a row moves the map to it and, on phones where the map sits above the column, scrolls back up to the map, and clicking a marker scrolls the list to its row. Tabs above the results column pick the layer for both the map and the column, in the order Customers, Competitors, Gap. Gap appears only once a campaign from the past 12 months has a station or address match. Customers opens at the start; when Gap appears it opens by itself unless another tab was clicked. Switching tabs slides the highlighted tab across, and the map's colours change at once. Ads with no fixed location are labelled citywide or borough-wide on their row, and the map's legend counts citywide campaigns as not pinned. - Built: competitor research with the Vercel AI SDK's `ToolLoopAgent`, OpenAI directly through `OPENAI_API_KEY`, and hosted web search and page reading. The research default is `gpt-5.6-luna`, selected in `AI_MODELS` in `web/src/lib/server/ai.ts`. Model names are not read from environment variables. Research runs in two stages. Discovery finds up to six competitors from the startup's product and target customer rather than its industry label: the model first writes short shopper phrases for the product and customer, then searches at least four times, recent campaign news first, for companies that have run New York subway, billboard, bus shelter, LinkNYC or wild-posting campaigns and that sell a substitute or target the same customer. Government campaigns, non-profits and media owners are excluded. Discovery also records every campaign its searches turn up, including ones that name no place, using recent campaigns and the current year in its searches. It cannot finish with fewer than three competitors until it has made five searches. Discovery has twelve model steps and a 60-second deadline. Each competitor then gets its own placement search for stations, street addresses, neighbourhoods and dates, told which campaigns are already recorded, with eight model steps and 65 seconds, all running concurrently within a 165-second total deadline; the Route Handler has a 170-second deadline. One finding can name up to six places, and each place becomes its own row. Each competitor can contribute up to six rows with a map location and three without, counted separately so that findings without a place cannot crowd out located ones. A run reports at most 54 rows. The first model step of each stage requires hosted search, and each response allows up to eight hosted tool calls, in parallel. Typed tools reject URLs absent from the run's research sources. Hosted-search-only responses continue into reporting within the same step budget. The model extracts the evidence; source membership does not independently fact-check its summary. Dates are classified as current, recent, historical or unknown. Current needs a date range covering today. Recent needs a source showing the campaign running within the past 12 months, using its start date, end date or latest evidence date. The agent may use a publication date as the latest evidence date only when the source reports the campaign as launching or running then, and a date given only as a month or year counts as the first day of that period. Only matched subway stations or street addresses with current or recent dates affect competition. Geocoded areas and unknown or historical dates remain visible as markers without lowering gap scores. Other findings stay visible with source links, evidence and locations. A search returning no campaigns does not establish that competitors are absent. The sample recording contains four competitors and twelve campaign findings, five of them with map locations, one of them recent at a street address so the recorded fallback can open the Gap tab. The Competitors tab ends with a "How the agent worked" section: the tools the agent called with counts (web search, open page, report competitor, report campaign, locate on map, and the two finish tools), then its reasoning summaries and each call in order, grouped by phase or competitor; the section folds away from its heading. Reasoning comes from OpenAI's `reasoningSummary: "auto"` and from the notes the model writes before searching; recorded research has no trace. Re-capture it from `tools/` with `uv run python -c 'from tools.sources.competitor_run import load; load(refresh=True)'` while the web app runs. - Built: campaign location lookup with Geoapify, using `GEOAPIFY_API_KEY` on the server, and street corners from a table of New York intersections. Supported source-reported landmarks, neighbourhoods and street addresses are resolved within New York. A neighbourhood gets an approximate marker at the neighbourhood itself. Geoapify results that are only the borough, a business named after the area, or a street that shares the area's words are rejected, because it returned Brooklyn itself for "Fort Greene, Brooklyn", a veterinary clinic for "Upper East Side, Manhattan" and a Bronx street for "West Village". Street corners, written as "A and B", "A & B", "A at B", "corner of A and B" or "A between B and C", are looked up in `data/streets/intersections.csv`, 55,978 intersections built by `tools/src/tools/sources/streets.py` from NYC Open Data's street centerline, and get address precision. A corner found in several boroughs is placed only when the text names one of them or a neighbourhood in it, and a corner not in the table stays unmapped; neither is sent to Geoapify, whose free-text search returned Madison Avenue & East 41st Street for "41st Street & 7th Avenue". A location naming only a borough stays unmapped. When Geoapify finds nothing for a place followed by its borough, such as "Times Square, Manhattan", it is asked again for the place alone.
