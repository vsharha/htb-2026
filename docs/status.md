- Built: onboarding form and results page, in `web/`, with live audience scoring and competitor research. Placement pricing remains mocked. The form is one fill-in-the-blank sentence ending in a city menu whose only city is New York, followed by New York's five boroughs as a fixed list. The results are a map of New York's census tracts with three layers, gap (the default), customers and competitors, with a legend and rings on stations with confirmed current competitor subway ads. The placements to buy are numbered markers that match their numbers in the list, shown on the gap and competitors layers but not the customers layer, because they are chosen from the gap. Hovering a placement on the map or in the list highlights it in both, clicking a row moves the map to it, and clicking a marker scrolls the list to its row. Tabs above the results column pick the layer for both the map and the column, in the order Customers, Competitors, Gap, so they read as the argument from customers to what competitors leave; Gap is selected at the start. Switching tabs slides the highlighted tab across, and the map's colours change at once. The column shows only that layer's sections: Gap has the 5 biggest gaps and 12 placements to buy, Customers has the 5 tracts with the most target customers and the weighted tract columns, strongest first, with those that count against the customer marked "less likely", and Competitors has the competitors and their ads, with the agent's latest step while it searches and a note counting the ads that matched no station and so aren't on the map. Submitting the form replaces it with the results filling the window: the map fills the left two thirds and stays in view while the column scrolls in the right third, and on phones the map sits on top at 55% of the screen height. The column opens with a one-line summary of the brief and an Edit button that brings the form back with its answers kept. `/map` shows the same results for a fixed sample brief when it loads, without the Edit button, to design the results without filling in the form. Faked: placement costs, measurement methods and minimum spends are invented. Real: the audience fit scores are weighted sums of the real scaled tract columns, and the placements are real kiosks and shelters in the 15 tracts with the biggest gaps. The column weights come from a local Clef decision model (Cloudflare `clef-flash` by default, Jev/SystemOne-compatible) at the `/v1/systemone` endpoint set by `CLEF_BASE_URL` in `web/.env.local` (default `http://127.0.0.1:30000`), run on the deployed cloud: one request asks a five-level score question per column, from strongly against to strongly for, and each weight runs from -1 to 1, the probability-weighted level. When the endpoint can't be reached, fails or takes over 20 seconds, the weights come from keyword matching on the target customer, and the Customers tab says so. The tract-combining logic was verified on 8 November 2026 with the sample brief on `/map` under the earlier Jev implementation (892 ms end to end for that run); Clef weighting needs to be verified against a running Clef server. The browser computes competition per tract from its distance to each confirmed current campaign's station, falling to about a third at 1 km, and the gap as audience fit times one minus competition; that formula is a first version, not a decision. The UI imports audience scoring, streamed competitor research and placement lookup through `web/src/lib/api.ts`, with types in `web/src/lib/contract.ts`. Placements appear after audience scoring and are re-ranked when research finishes. On 7 November 2026, the live energy-drink flow returned three competitors and three source-linked campaign findings. Their dates or station locations were unknown, so none lowered the gap scores. The production sample fallback showed three recorded competitors and its capture-date label; a different brief received no recorded findings. Both browser flows had no console errors. Verified in `2a85115`. Serves G-Research and HRT.
- Built: competitor research with the Vercel AI SDK's `ToolLoopAgent`, OpenAI directly through `OPENAI_API_KEY`, and hosted web search and page reading. The research default is `gpt-5-mini`, selected in `AI_MODELS` in `web/src/lib/server/ai.ts`. Model names are not read from environment variables. Typed tools report up to three competitors and six campaign locations, rejecting URLs absent from the run's research sources. The SDK controls the loop with a 16-step limit and a 165-second timeout; the Route Handler has a 170-second deadline. The model extracts the evidence; source membership does not independently fact-check its summary. Dates are classified as current, historical or unknown. Only explicit, unambiguous subway station matches with a confirmed date range covering today affect competition. Other findings stay visible with source links, evidence and locations. A search returning no campaigns does not establish that competitors are absent. The sample recording contains three competitors and no verified campaigns. Re-capture it from `tools/` with `uv run python -c 'from tools.sources.competitor_run import load; load(refresh=True)'` while the web app runs. Verified on 7 November 2026 in `2a85115`: live research and production fallback flows above; eight focused tests for CSV parsing, source checks, station matching, dates, gap effects, split SDK stream chunks, interruption and cancellation; HTTP 400 for invalid briefs and 413 for oversized briefs; web lint, typecheck and build; Python Ruff checks; station data and recording present in the production deployment trace. Serves G-Research. Search and page reading alone do not establish the browser-control workflow for Wordsmith.
