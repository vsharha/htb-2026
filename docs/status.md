# Status

Each feature is listed as built, mocked, planned or cut, with the tracks it serves.

- Built: records how it was verified, as the flow, the result, the date and the commit.
- Mocked: says what is faked and what the real version would need.
- Cut: keeps its line, with the reason.

- Cut: Revolut mispricing check with real data. Recomputes the source's airport figures from published prices, audience counts and attention studies, with attention seconds and the Meta price as sliders. Serves G-Research. Cut on 7 November 2026 by the pivot to the audience heatmap, which leaves out airports.
- Cut: airport placement ranking per startup. Ranks UK and EU airport placements by how well each airport's route and airline mix matches the startup's target customer, with published or estimated prices and a sent enquiry; two startups are compared side by side in the demo. Serves G-Research and Wordsmith. Cut on 7 November 2026 by the pivot to the audience heatmap, which leaves out airports.
- Mocked: onboarding form and results page, in `web/`, with stand-in server calls. The form is one fill-in-the-blank sentence ending in a city menu whose only city is New York, followed by New York's five boroughs as a fixed list. The results are a map of New York's census tracts with three layers, gap (the default), customers and competitors, with a legend and rings on the stations where competitors' ads were found. The placements to buy are numbered markers that match their numbers in the list, shown on the gap and competitors layers but not the customers layer, because they are chosen from the gap. Hovering a placement on the map or in the list highlights it in both, clicking a row moves the map to it, and clicking a marker scrolls the list to its row. Tabs above the results column pick the layer for both the map and the column, and the column shows only that layer's sections: Gap has the 5 biggest gaps and 12 placements to buy, Customers has the 5 tracts with the most target customers and the weighted tract columns, and Competitors has the competitors and their ads, with the agent's latest step while it searches and a note counting the ads that matched no station and so aren't on the map. Submitting the form replaces it with the results filling the window: the map fills the left two thirds and stays in view while the column scrolls in the right third, and on phones the map sits on top at 55% of the screen height. The column opens with a one-line summary of the brief and an Edit button that brings the form back with its answers kept. `/map` shows the same results for a fixed sample brief when it loads, without the Edit button, to design the results without filling in the form. Faked: Jev's column weights come from keyword matching on the target customer; the competitor agent replays a script with placeholder competitors "Competitor A" and "Competitor B", example.com source links and six real subway stations; placement costs, measurement methods and minimum spends are invented. Real: the audience fit scores are weighted sums of the real scaled tract columns, and the placements are real kiosks and shelters in the 15 tracts with the biggest gaps. The browser computes competition per tract from its distance to each campaign's station, falling to about a third at 1 km, and the gap as audience fit times one minus competition; that formula is a first version, not a decision. Swapping in the real backend means changing the exports in `web/src/lib/api.ts`, which match the types in `web/src/lib/contract.ts`. Serves G-Research, Wordsmith and HRT.
- Planned: New York audience gap map. Maps where a startup's target customers live, work and pass through, where competitors' outdoor campaigns already reach them, and the gap between the two, then lists placements to buy in the gap areas. Serves G-Research, Wordsmith (the competitor-campaign agent) and HRT.
