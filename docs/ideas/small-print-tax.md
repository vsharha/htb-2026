# Small Print Tax: the real cost of a broadband, mobile or energy contract

Status: proposed, 7 November 2026.

## Idea

The advertised monthly price is not what you pay. The agent reads your contract and finds what really drives the cost: built-in price rises, exit fees, and the jump when an introductory discount ends. It draws a 24-month total-cost chart with each step labelled by the clause that causes it, and predicts what the provider will quote at renewal. Then it fetches the live quote to compare. It ranks switching options by cost, contract length, speed and green credentials, and drafts a switch or haggle script.

## Evidence and data

- Confirmed: Ofcom's rule that price rises must be stated in pounds and pence applies from 17 January 2025, but not retrospectively. Older contracts can still carry inflation-linked rises.

## Tracks it could serve

- Bank of Scotland: strong. Cost, time, convenience and sustainability, exactly as the brief puts it.
- G-Research: strong. The hidden "small print tax" becomes a visible chart.
- HRT: moderate. A renewal prediction checked against a live quote, using inflation data.
- Wordsmith: moderate. Contract review is close to legal work, and the agent visits several sites.

## Challenges it could use

- Gemini API: reading contracts.
- Tiger Data: price and inflation time series.

## Open problems

- Comparison sites block bots and ban scraping, so the live quote may fail at the table.
- Ofcom puts the broadband loyalty penalty at only about £108 a year.
- A wrong prediction live is embarrassing, so rehearse with addresses already checked.
- Bill-saving apps are common at hackathons.

## Scope

Upload a contract advertised at "£25/mo". A 24-month chart climbs to an effective £34/mo. The predicted renewal quote appears, then the live quote is checked against it. End on a ranked switch table.
