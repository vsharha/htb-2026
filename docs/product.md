# Product

## What it is

The ad channel mispricing finder tells a startup where to advertise to reach its customers for less than the channels its competitors crowd into. A startup fills in a short form about its product, customer, budget and markets. It gets back a ranked list of specific placements at UK and EU airports, such as Hamburg Airport's jet bridges, baggage reclaim screens or lounge advertising. Each comes with a published or estimated price and a contact, a cost comparison against Meta, a way to measure results, and a drafted enquiry to the seller. Airports are the first group of channels; others, such as students' unions and newsletters, can be added later by adding an agent for that medium.

It is for startups that want customers beyond Meta and Google but have no media agency and a limited budget.

Three things set it apart from AI media planners and ad marketplaces:

- It ranks placements for each startup by who passes through them, so two startups with different customers get different airports, and it leaves out placements the startup's competitors already buy.
- It ranks placements by cost per thousand attentive seconds weighted by audience value, not by cost per thousand impressions. The source behind the idea shows the ranking flips: Berlin Brandenburg's jet bridges are about 9% dearer than Meta per impression but 2.78 times cheaper per attentive second, assuming four seconds of attention.
- It shows a published rate card where one exists. Where the price is "on request", it shows an estimate, labelled as one, and sends the seller an enquiry to get a real quote.

## Decisions

Every decision the user makes or confirms, including ones reported from teammates: who the product serves, data sources, methods or models, the stack, how parts connect, and the demo scope. Track and challenge priorities are recorded in `AGENTS.md`, not here. One line each: the decision, the reason, and the date. A changed decision is updated in place, so this section always describes the current plan.

- Product idea: the ad channel mispricing finder, which finds advertising channels that are underpriced for the attention and audience they deliver; details in `ideas/ad-channel-mispricing.md`. The user chose it over the other brainstormed ideas. 2026-11-07.
- Web app: Next.js 16 (App Router, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS 4, ESLint) managed with pnpm, in the `web/` folder rather than the repo root. The repo root stays for docs and agent config, and a second service such as a Python API can sit beside `web/` later. 2026-11-07.
- Frontend UI: shadcn/ui components styled with Tailwind CSS v4. 2026-11-07.
- LLM calls and AI agents: Vercel AI SDK. 2026-11-07.
- Recommend specific placements, such as Hamburg Airport's jet bridges, not mediums such as "airports". Per impression, airports as a medium are no cheaper than Meta (BER's jet bridges cost £14.24 per thousand against Meta's £13.02), so any underpricing is in individual placements, and a placement is something a startup can actually buy. 2026-11-07.
- The pipeline has six steps: crowded-channel check, candidates, gap filter, verification by per-medium agents, pricing, then report and outreach. Details in `plan/architecture-sketch.md`. 2026-11-07.
- Per-medium expert agents, such as an airport agent or a student union agent, verify candidates rather than propose them. Each medium is sold differently, and without a check the model invents plausible channels that don't exist or have no price. Only the airport agent is built for now. 2026-11-07.
- Airports are the first and only group of channels for now. Candidates are any advertising placement at a UK or EU airport, such as jet bridges, baggage reclaim, security trays, lounges and Wi-Fi login pages. Airports carry the Revolut evidence and have strong public audience data; other groups become the roadmap. 2026-11-07.
- Audience value per airport comes from passenger volume and flight data. Volume comes from published counts (CAA for UK airports, Eurostat for EU airports, airport media kits), not from flights. The mix of routes and airlines (business routes and full-service airlines against low-cost and charter) sets how well an airport's passengers match the startup's target customer. Volume per jet bridge from flights per gate is used only if gate data can be gathered. Published counts are more accurate for volume, while the route and airline mix is what distinguishes one airport's audience from another's. 2026-11-07.
- Where a placement's price is "on request", the price is estimated from Hamburg Airport's published jet-bridge rate per passenger (€33,000 a month for 16 jet bridges, 13.6 million passengers in 2024), scaled by the airport's passengers and route mix, and labelled as an estimate. The enquiry sent in the outreach step is how the estimate gets confirmed. Most airport prices are unpublished, and quotes won't arrive before the demo. 2026-11-07.
- Placements are ranked by cost per thousand attentive seconds, weighted by audience value, against a Meta baseline, shown beside cost per thousand impressions. Attention seconds and the Meta price are sliders with the breakeven point marked, because no attention data exists for most placements. 2026-11-07.
- A channel counts as crowded if competitors advertise there or if we have already recommended it to another client in the same category. For airport placements, competitors are found by an agent searching the web and press (for example "<competitor> airport advertising"), because ad transparency libraries such as Meta's Ad Library and Google's Ads Transparency Center cover only online ads. Recommending what similar startups use would send every client to the channels where competition has already raised prices. 2026-11-07.
- The onboarding form asks for industry, a one-sentence description, a one-sentence target customer, what a customer pays, monthly advertising budget, and markets. It does not ask B2B or B2C, which the target customer covers. Budget filters out placements with minimum spends, and what a customer pays stands in for lifetime value in the audience weighting because it is easier to answer. 2026-11-07.
- Every recommendation comes with a way to measure it, such as a QR code, promo code or landing page, because marketers avoid channels they can't measure. 2026-11-07.
- Demo scope: confirm the Revolut airport mispricing with real data, by checking the source's figures (cost per thousand impressions and per thousand attentive seconds against Meta, and the breakeven attention) against published prices, audience counts and attention studies. The source's model rests on assumptions, so the aim is to test them. 2026-11-07.
- Demo scope: run two startups with different target customers side by side, such as a fintech for business travellers and a travel app for students, and show that they get different airport rankings. This shows the product chooses placements per startup rather than reading out a fixed list of airports. 2026-11-07.
- Airport data is gathered ahead of time with scripts in `tools/`, and the live agents add to it. A recorded run of the full pipeline is kept as a fallback for the demo, because live browser agents fail on cookie banners, PDFs and slow sites. 2026-11-07.
